Your Journey Towards Parenthood Starts With Understanding

Infertility can affect either partner or both partners. Identifying the underlying cause is the first step towards selecting an appropriate fertility treatment.

What Is Infertility?

Infertility is generally defined as failure to achieve pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sexual intercourse. It may be due to female factors, male factors, combined factors or unexplained infertility.

Important: Infertility is not solely a woman's problem. Both partners should undergo appropriate evaluation together. Earlier assessment is appropriate when there are known risk factors or increasing maternal age.

The World Health Organization estimates that approximately one in six people of reproductive age experience infertility during their lifetime. Read WHO information.

Common Causes of Infertility in Couples

There are several medical, hormonal, anatomical and lifestyle-related reasons why couples may experience difficulty conceiving.

1. Ovulation Disorders

Irregular or absent egg release may prevent natural conception.

  • PCOS
  • Thyroid disorders
  • High prolactin levels
  • Hypothalamic disorders
  • Premature ovarian insufficiency
Read about female factors →

2. Tubal Factors

Blocked or damaged fallopian tubes can prevent sperm and egg from meeting.

  • Previous pelvic infections
  • Pelvic tuberculosis
  • Previous abdominal surgery
  • Endometriosis
  • Previous ectopic pregnancy

3. Uterine Factors

Conditions affecting the uterine cavity may interfere with implantation.

  • Uterine fibroids
  • Endometrial polyps
  • Intrauterine adhesions
  • Septate uterus
  • Congenital uterine abnormalities

4. Male Infertility

Abnormal sperm production, function or transport may contribute to infertility.

  • Low sperm count
  • Poor sperm motility
  • Abnormal sperm morphology
  • Varicocele
  • Obstructive azoospermia
Read about male factors →

5. Age-Related Infertility

Female fertility generally decreases with increasing age due to declining egg quantity and quality.

  • Reduced ovarian reserve
  • Age-related egg abnormalities
  • Increased miscarriage risk

6. Unexplained Infertility

Sometimes routine investigations do not identify a definite cause despite difficulty conceiving.

  • Normal ovulation
  • Apparently normal tubes
  • Normal semen parameters
  • No identifiable explanation after evaluation

Female Infertility: Causes and Treatment

PCOS – Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

PCOS may cause irregular ovulation, irregular periods and difficulty conceiving.

Management may include:
  • Weight and lifestyle management when appropriate
  • Ovulation induction
  • Monitoring follicular development
  • IUI or IVF when indicated
PCOS patient information

Low Ovarian Reserve

Reduced ovarian reserve means fewer remaining eggs. It does not independently determine whether natural conception is possible.

Management may include:
  • AMH and antral follicle count assessment
  • Individualized treatment planning
  • IVF where appropriate
  • Discussion of available reproductive options

Blocked Fallopian Tubes

Tubal damage may occur following infection, surgery or endometriosis.

Management may include:
  • HSG or other tubal assessment
  • Selected tubal surgery
  • IVF for significant tubal disease

Endometriosis

Endometriosis can affect pelvic anatomy, ovarian function and fertility.

Management may include:
  • Ultrasound evaluation
  • Selected laparoscopic treatment
  • Fertility planning according to age and ovarian reserve
  • IUI or IVF in selected cases

Uterine Fibroids and Polyps

Some fibroids and endometrial polyps can affect the uterine cavity and implantation.

Management may include:
  • Ultrasound and cavity assessment
  • Hysteroscopic polypectomy
  • Selected myomectomy

Premature Ovarian Insufficiency

Reduced ovarian function before 40 years of age may cause irregular or absent periods and reduced fertility.

Management may include:
  • Hormonal and ovarian assessment
  • Early fertility counselling
  • Discussion of appropriate assisted reproduction options, including donor oocytes where indicated

Male Infertility: Causes and Treatment

Male factors contribute to a substantial proportion of infertility cases. Semen analysis is an important initial investigation.

Low Sperm Count – Oligozoospermia

Lower-than-expected sperm concentration may reduce the chance of natural conception.

Management:
  • Repeat semen analysis when indicated
  • Hormonal evaluation
  • Treatment of reversible causes
  • IUI or IVF/ICSI depending on severity

Poor Sperm Motility

Reduced sperm movement can interfere with sperm reaching and fertilizing the egg.

Management:
  • Evaluate underlying causes
  • Assess lifestyle and medical history
  • Consider IUI or IVF/ICSI where appropriate

Azoospermia

Azoospermia refers to the absence of sperm in the ejaculate and requires evaluation to distinguish obstructive and non-obstructive causes.

Management:
  • Hormonal and genetic assessment when indicated
  • Evaluation for obstruction
  • Selected surgical sperm retrieval
  • IVF with ICSI when appropriate

Varicocele

Dilated veins around the testicle may be associated with abnormal semen parameters in some men.

Management:
  • Clinical examination
  • Semen analysis
  • Selected varicocele treatment
  • Further fertility treatment when required

Hormonal Male Infertility

Disorders affecting testosterone, FSH, LH and other hormones may interfere with sperm production.

Management:
  • Hormonal evaluation
  • Cause-specific medical treatment
  • Specialist andrology consultation

Erectile or Ejaculatory Problems

Sexual and ejaculatory dysfunction can prevent effective sperm delivery.

Management:
  • Identify underlying causes
  • Medical or specialist management
  • Assisted reproductive options when indicated

Infertility Investigations for Couples

The investigations are selected according to the age, medical history, symptoms and duration of infertility.

Female Investigations

  • Menstrual and reproductive history
  • Pelvic and transvaginal ultrasound
  • AMH where appropriate
  • Antral follicle count
  • Hormonal tests as indicated
  • HSG or other tubal patency assessment
  • Uterine cavity assessment when needed

Male Investigations

  • Semen analysis
  • Clinical examination
  • FSH, LH and testosterone when indicated
  • Scrotal ultrasound when indicated
  • Genetic investigations in selected cases
  • Further evaluation for azoospermia

Couple-Based Evaluation

  • Duration of infertility
  • Previous pregnancy history
  • Previous IUI or IVF history
  • Sexual and lifestyle history
  • Recurrent pregnancy loss history
  • Individualized fertility planning

Available Fertility Treatment Approaches

Treatment should be individualized. Not every couple requires IVF; simpler treatments may be appropriate depending on the diagnosis.

1. Ovulation Induction

Medications may be used to help women with ovulatory dysfunction release an egg. Ultrasound monitoring may be required.

2. IUI – Intrauterine Insemination

Prepared sperm is placed inside the uterus around ovulation. IUI may be suitable in selected cases with adequate sperm parameters and appropriate tubal status.

3. IVF – In Vitro Fertilization

Eggs are collected from the ovaries, fertilized with sperm in a laboratory and resulting embryos may be transferred to the uterus.

4. ICSI – Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection

A selected sperm is injected into an egg. ICSI is commonly considered for significant male-factor infertility and certain previous fertilization problems.

5. Surgical Fertility Treatment

Selected patients may benefit from hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, treatment of fibroids, adhesions, endometriosis or other correctable conditions.

6. Donor Gamete Treatment

Donor oocytes or sperm may be considered in appropriate circumstances, following counselling and applicable Indian ART laws and regulations.

7. Fertility Preservation

Egg, sperm or embryo freezing may be considered in selected circumstances, including before certain medical treatments.

8. Unexplained Infertility Management

Depending on age, duration of infertility and previous treatment, options may include expectant management, ovarian stimulation with IUI or IVF.

When Should a Couple Consult a Fertility Specialist?

Below 35 years

Consider evaluation after 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse if pregnancy has not occurred.

35 years and above

Consider evaluation after 6 months of unsuccessful attempts.

Above 40 years

Earlier assessment is generally advisable.

Known medical problems

Seek earlier evaluation for irregular periods, endometriosis, previous pelvic infection, tubal disease, known male-factor problems or previous chemotherapy.
